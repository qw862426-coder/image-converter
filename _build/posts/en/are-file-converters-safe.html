Most converters upload your file

Most free online converters work like this: your file travels over the internet to the company’s server, is converted there, and you download the result. It is convenient, but it means your file sits on someone else’s computer for a while.

Well-run services say they delete files after a set time, but you can’t check that yourself. For sensitive files such as ID cards, contracts, bank statements or medical papers, it is worth thinking twice.

What to check before you use one

  • A privacy policy: does it say how long files are kept and when they are deleted?
  • Who runs it and how to contact them: avoid sites with no owner information.
  • An address starting with https: the connection your file travels over is encrypted.
  • No strange installs: be careful if a site asks you to install a program or allow notifications to convert a file.
  • Ads that look like download buttons: make sure you press the real save button.

Some converters don’t upload at all

Modern browsers are powerful enough to do many conversions inside the browser instead of on a server. With this approach, your file never leaves your device.

In short

  • For everyday photos and public material, either approach is fine.
  • For sensitive documents, use a converter that doesn’t upload, or a program installed on your own computer.
  • Whatever you use, keep the converted files somewhere safe.
