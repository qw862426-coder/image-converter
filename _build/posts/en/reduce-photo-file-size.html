Why are photos so big?

A phone photo is usually 3–10 MB, because it has many pixels and is saved at high quality. Email attachments, job applications, school portals and government forms often limit uploads to 2 MB or 5 MB.

There are two ways to make a photo smaller:

  • Lower the quality (compression): remove detail you can barely see. The width and height stay the same.
  • Make the picture smaller: reduce the number of pixels. Halving a 4,000-pixel photo to 2,000 pixels cuts the file size a lot.

What quality should you choose?

QualityHow it looksGood for
90% or moreAlmost identical to the originalPrinting, keeping
70–85%Differences only up close; much smaller filesForms, email, blogs
Below 50%Blocky patches start to show in skies and skinOnly when size really matters

Start at 70%, look at the result, then go up or down.

How to do it with PicShifter

  1. Open Compress images.
  2. Choose your photos. Several at once is fine.
  3. Quality is set to 70%. Adjust it if you need to.
  4. Press Convert. Each file shows the old size, the new size and the percentage saved.

For pictures on your own website, choose WebP as the format. It is usually smaller than JPG at the same quality.

Common problems

The file got bigger

If the original was already heavily compressed, or is a small PNG graphic, saving it again may not help. Keep the original in that case.

What about screenshots with text?

Strong JPG compression can blur the edges of letters. Keep quality at 85% or higher, or keep the PNG.

I need to send several photos as one file

You can combine them into one PDF and shrink them at the same time. On JPG to PDF, lower the quality to about 70%.

Remember

  • Keep your originals. A compressed photo can’t be turned back into the original quality.
  • Before you submit, open the smaller photo full size and check that faces and text are clear.
