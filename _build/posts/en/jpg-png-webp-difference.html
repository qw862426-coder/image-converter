JPG: use it for photos

JPG makes files small by dropping detail you can barely see (“lossy” compression). Photos stay small and look good, but quality drops a little each time you re-save, and transparent areas turn white. It is the safe choice for phone photos, forms and email.

PNG: use it for text and sharp lines

PNG keeps every pixel exactly (“lossless”). Text in screenshots and lines in logos stay crisp, and it supports transparency. Saving a photo as PNG, however, can make it several times larger than a JPG.

WebP: use it on websites

WebP is a format Google introduced in 2010 to make websites load faster. It can compress like JPG or save losslessly like PNG, and it supports transparency. At the same quality it is usually smaller than JPG.

The catch is that some places still can’t open it, including some photo editors, office programs and upload forms. If an image you saved from the web is a .webp that won’t open, convert it with WebP to JPG.

A simple rule

  1. A photo → JPG
  2. Text, a logo, or you need a transparent background → PNG
  3. An image for your own website → WebP
  4. Not sure where it will be opened → JPG (the safest)

How to change the format

In the image converter, choose your images and pick the format. Common conversions also have their own pages: PNG to JPG, JPG to PNG, WebP to JPG.

One thing to remember: turning a JPG into a PNG doesn’t improve quality. Detail that was dropped doesn’t come back; the file just gets bigger.
