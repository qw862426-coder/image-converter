Why “one PDF”?

A pile of separate photos has to be opened one by one, and pages get mixed up or lost. One PDF keeps every page in order in a single file, which is why expense claims, school paperwork and applications often ask for it.

How to combine photos

  1. Open JPG to PDF.
  2. Choose your photos in page order. The order you add them is the order of the pages.
  3. Check that Combine everything into one PDF file is on. Turn it off to get a separate PDF for each photo.
  4. Press Convert and save the PDF.

Each page takes the shape of its photo, so nothing is stretched or cropped. HEIC and PNG photos can be mixed in too.

When there is a size limit

Six full-size phone photos can make a 20–30 MB PDF. If there is a size limit, make it again with quality at about 70%. Text usually stays readable and the file gets much smaller.

If the photos are of paper documents

Photos of receipts or forms on a desk are tilted, shadowed and show the desk around them. Use Photo to scan instead: it finds the edges of the paper, straightens the page, removes shadows and makes one PDF.

Check before you submit

  • Open the PDF and check the page order.
  • Zoom in to make sure small text such as amounts and dates can be read.
  • Give the file a clear name, such as “Name_Document.pdf”.

Questions

What happens to transparent PNGs?

Transparent areas become white in the PDF.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. The PDF is made inside your browser and the photos never leave your device. That matters most for sensitive images such as ID cards and bank statements.
