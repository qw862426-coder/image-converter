Why compress photos

Phone photos are often 3–10 MB each. Email attachments, job and school applications and website uploads frequently have limits of a few megabytes. Saving a photo as JPG at a lower quality removes detail you can barely see and can make the file several times smaller.

How to compress

  1. Choose your photos above.
  2. The quality is set to 70%, which usually shrinks files a lot with little visible change. Go lower for smaller files, higher for sharper ones.
  3. For website images, choose WebP in the format menu. It is usually smaller than JPG at the same quality.
  4. Press Convert. Each file shows the old size, the new size and the percentage saved.

Frequently asked questions

How small can I go?

Below about 50% you may start to see blocky patches, especially in skies and skin. Try a few settings and compare.

Does this change the width and height?

No. The image keeps its size in pixels; only the file gets smaller.

Why did a file get bigger?

If the original was already heavily compressed, or was a small PNG graphic, re-saving may not help. Keep the original in that case.
