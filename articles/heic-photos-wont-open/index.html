iPhone photos (HEIC) won’t open on Windows? Here’s how to fix it

You copied photos from your iPhone and Windows says it can’t open them. The cause is the HEIC format, and there are four simple fixes.

Published 2026-11-26

Why won’t they open?

Since iOS 11 in 2017, iPhones save photos as HEIC by default. HEIC keeps about the same quality as JPG in a smaller file, which saves space on your phone.

The trouble is that many places still can’t read it: some Windows programs, job and government upload forms, photo print shops and older Android phones. So when a file name ends in .heic, you often get a “can’t open this file” message.

Fix 1. Convert the photos you already have to JPG (fastest)

If the photos are already on your computer, turning them into JPG is the simplest fix. JPG opens on almost every device and website.

  1. Open the HEIC to JPG converter.
  2. Choose your HEIC photos. You can pick many at once.
  3. Press Convert, then Save all.

The photos are converted inside your browser and never uploaded. At 90% quality or higher, the difference is very hard to see.

Fix 2. Let the iPhone convert when you copy to a computer

If you copy photos to a computer with a cable, one iPhone setting converts them to JPG as they are copied.

  • On the iPhone, go to Settings → Photos and set Transfer to Mac or PC to Automatic.

Photos are then copied in a compatible format (JPG). This may not apply to original files you send by email or messaging apps.

Fix 3. Take new photos as JPG from the start

  • Go to Settings → Camera → Formats and choose Most Compatible.

New photos are saved as JPG. Each photo takes more space, and photos you already took stay HEIC.

Fix 4. Open HEIC directly on Windows

On Windows 10 and 11 you can install HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store to view HEIC photos in the Photos app. Some photos also need HEVC Video Extensions, which may cost a small fee. This only helps you view them on your own PC; to upload or send them, JPG is still easier.
